Painless Root Canals

Modern Therapy to Relieve Pain and Save Your Natural Teeth

End Your Toothache, Keep Your Tooth

The term "root canal" can cause anxiety, but modern root canal therapy is a routine, pain-relieving procedure. Its primary goal is to save a tooth that is severely infected or decayed, preventing the need for extraction. At Seva Dental Clinic, we use advanced techniques and anesthesia to ensure your treatment is as comfortable and stress-free as possible.

When is a Root Canal Needed?

A root canal is necessary when the soft tissue inside your tooth, known as the pulp, becomes inflamed or infected. This can be caused by deep decay, a crack or chip in the tooth, or repeated dental procedures. Symptoms often include severe toothache, prolonged sensitivity to heat or cold, and swelling.

The Seva Root Canal Process

  1. Diagnosis and Numbing: We confirm the need for a root canal with an X-ray and then completely numb the area to ensure you feel no pain.
  2. Cleaning the Canal: A small opening is made in the tooth, and the damaged pulp is carefully removed. The inside of the tooth is then cleaned and disinfected.
  3. Filling and Sealing: The cleaned space is filled with a biocompatible material and sealed to prevent re-infection.
  4. Restoration: In most cases, a dental crown is placed over the treated tooth to protect it and restore its full function and appearance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a root canal actually painful?

No. The procedure itself is not painful as it's performed under local anesthesia. The pain people associate with root canals is actually the pain from the infection *before* the procedure. The root canal is what relieves that pain.

Why not just pull the tooth out?

Saving your natural tooth is always the best option. It helps maintain proper spacing in your jaw, preserves bone, and allows for normal chewing function. A missing tooth can lead to other dental problems over time.

What happens after the root canal?

Your tooth may be a little sensitive for a few days, but this is manageable. You will need to return for a final restoration, usually a crown, to protect the tooth and ensure it lasts for many years to come.
